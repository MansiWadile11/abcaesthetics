What to know before your first Botox® or filler treatment

Neuromodulators, dermal fillers and biostimulators do different jobs. Here is how the categories differ, and how the choice is made with you at consultation.

1. Neuromodulators and fillers do different jobs

They are often spoken about together, but they work on different things:

  • Neuromodulators — Botox®, Dysport®, Xeomin® and Daxxify® are botulinum toxin type A treatments used to temporarily reduce or improve the appearance of selected dynamic facial lines and wrinkles.
  • Dermal fillers — the JUVÉDERM®, Restylane® and RHA® Collections are hyaluronic acid fillers, selected according to treatment area, facial anatomy and individual aesthetic goals.
  • Biostimulators — Sculptra® is designed to stimulate collagen production and provide gradual improvement in selected areas; Radiesse® is used for selected facial contouring and structural enhancement.

Which category suits you follows from your goals rather than from a product being popular.

2. The consultation comes first

Treatment recommendations, eligibility, treatment frequency and product selection are individualized following consultation and clinical assessment. That conversation covers your goals, your relevant medical history, and an assessment of your facial anatomy.

You should expect to leave a consultation understanding your options well enough to make an informed decision, including the option of not proceeding.

3. What “natural-looking” means here

The aim is to enhance your natural features while keeping the result balanced. That usually means treating selectively rather than extensively, and reviewing how you respond before deciding anything further.

4. Results vary, and no outcome is guaranteed

Injectable treatments carry potential risks and side effects, which are discussed with you before treatment so you can make an informed decision about your care. Results vary by individual and no specific outcome is guaranteed.

Dosing, injection placement and clinical protocols are determined by the treating provider based on individual assessment, which is why you will not find fixed numbers for them here.

“Treatment planning is individualized rather than applied as a one-size-fits-all model. Product selection, treatment areas and sequencing are decided following consultation and clinical assessment.”

Conclusion

Injectables are a category, not a single treatment. Knowing what each product is for makes the consultation more useful — you can talk about the change you want rather than the brand you have heard of, and let the clinical assessment decide the rest.
